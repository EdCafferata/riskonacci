## 1 — Waarde (EN) · "Story points ignore half the story"
Your team agrees: 5 points.
Nobody says out loud that there's a 50/50 chance the external API won't be ready.

Story points measure effort. They don't measure risk.
And risk is usually what blows up a sprint.

What helps: after the estimate, do one more quick round.
→ How likely is it to go wrong? (1–5)
→ How bad would it be? (1–5)

Two numbers, 60 seconds, and the risky item is suddenly visible for the whole team.

How does your team make risk visible during refinement?

## 3 — Waarde (EN) · Likelihood × Impact in 3 sentences
Likelihood × Impact, explained for refinement sessions:

1. Likelihood = how probable it is that this item hits a snag.
2. Impact = how much damage that snag does to the sprint goal.
3. High × high = split it, spike it, or tackle it first — don't just "estimate it bigger".

Bigger estimates hide risk. A matrix shows it.

## 5 — Waarde (EN) · The loudest estimate
The most dangerous estimate in planning poker isn't the wrong one.
It's the one said out loud before everyone voted.

Anchoring is real: once the senior dev says "that's a 3", the cards magically converge on 3.

Rules that help:
• Everyone votes at the same time. No exceptions.
• Highest and lowest explain first, not the most senior person.
• Re-vote once. Not five times.

What's your team's rule?

## 8 — Waarde (EN) · T-shirt vs Fibonacci
T-shirt sizes or Fibonacci?

T-shirt (S/M/L/XL): great early on, for roadmap items and when the team is new to estimating. Nobody argues about 5 vs 8.
Fibonacci: better once the backlog is refined and you want velocity.
Risk deck: use it on top of either — for the items where "how big?" isn't the real question.

Riskonacci has all three. But the method matters more than the tool.

## 10 — Remote teams (EN)
Remote refinement tip: stop estimating in chat.

"I'd say 5" in Slack isn't planning poker — the first number anchors everyone else.

Use anything that reveals cards at the same time. (Riskonacci does this realtime on iPhone, iPad and Mac, free — but a hidden-vote Google Form works too.)

The point is simultaneous reveal, not the app.
